# Compositional policy

**Definition.** A policy that is a property of the whole execution trace — authority limits, referral thresholds, cumulative caps, required review — evaluated by recomputing the guarded quantity from raw provenance, not by trusting any single step's derived field.

**Contrast.** Step-level compliance is not workflow compliance. Compositional Policy Violations (2609.18820): every individual step can pass its classifier, per-turn rail, or span evaluator while the composed run violates the policy the organization actually holds. Four types, each named by the mutation site: Authority Creep (permissions accumulate and reroute later steps), Threshold Laundering (an early check is not repeated after the quantity moves), Cumulative Sum Violation (legal atoms exceed an aggregate cap — three $90 buys against a $250 daily limit), Context Collapse (faithful summaries drift; context-setting facts 25% → 9%). A predicate over one step cannot see a property that step does not determine, so better step monitors do not close the class. ContrAgent (2609.18128): the missing artifact is one deterministic contract that both gates the next tool call and scores the finished trace. Assume-guarantee contracts in LTLf compile to a DFA; the contract library is independent of the model. Matches LLM-judge and rule-based guardrail baselines on four benches with reproducible verdicts and much lower online per-call latency. Pair with runtime-consequence-control: OATS / Scan the Skill stop one prohibited *class*; this stops a *legal* class that only becomes illegal in composition. Pair with evals-gates / VP-CONTROL: two models voting on one file are still one factorization; the DFA is a second source only if it reads raw events. Pair with authorization-laundering: EAL invents a grant inside the store; CPV spends a real grant past its cap.
