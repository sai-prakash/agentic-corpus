# 2026-10-26

Agensh (arXiv:2609.26781, Microsoft Research, open-sourced 24 Oct): self-organized multi-agent harness without a central orchestrator. Concurrent workers gather context, claim sub-tasks, share findings, verify and merge asynchronously via shared Gitea workspace, Mattermost, and context board. On the five hardest ProgramBench tasks (GPT-5.6-sol high, 6 h offline): mean final test-pass rate from 19.31% (1 agent) to 28.78% (128). On pandoc: 33.89% → 55.06% (1,024). Larger teams reach rates sooner; specialized roles emerge. Contrast: multi-agent coordination is not always a lead agent; shared infrastructure + self-claim scales. Heat 4. Concept written. Strong independent X signal (@Marktechpost bookmarks ~229; multiple others).

RiseHarness (arXiv:2610.27435, submitted 20 Oct, Wang et al.): framework for iteratively improving long-horizon terminal agent harnesses while keeping model weights fixed. Focuses on flipping tasks (both successful and failed runs under the same config), combines agent trajectories with internal harness traces from a runtime monitor, uses a Harness Handbook to link evidence to behaviors and source code, and adapts designs from other frameworks. Improves task success and reliability (e.g., Pass@4 gains on Terminal-Bench variants). Extends harness-evolution with dual-trace analysis. Heat 4.

OO-Harness (arXiv:2610.28380, submitted 20 Oct, Li et al.): evolves a hierarchy of specialized executable harness classes via object-oriented inheritance. Evolution agent creates subclasses from execution records; at test time a router selects using accumulated routing lessons (or reuses base). Up to +15.79 pp absolute on long-horizon benchmarks (AutomationBench, Workspace-Bench, Toolathlon). Specialists earn the route. Heat 4.

Anti-Persistence (arXiv:2610.27406, submitted 19 Oct, Wang et al.): memory design that accumulates trajectories and outcomes but reconstructs task-specific guidance on demand and discards it after use. Prevents error reproduction through abstraction-execution-reabstraction cycles. Higher overall held-out success and lower LLM-call cost than stored-abstraction baselines across benchmarks and backbones. Contrast: more memory of synthesized rules can make agents worse than raw trajectories. Related to Raw-RAG baseline theme. Heat 4.
