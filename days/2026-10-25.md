# 2026-10-25

Raw-RAG (arXiv:2610.30755, submitted 22 Oct, Bao et al.): naive dense retrieval + reranking over raw conversation history ranks first in 6 of 7 benchmark-model settings across LoCoMo, LongMemEval, MEME, and MemoryAgentBench, beating the strongest compared agentic memory system by 0.13–5.53 points. Higher evidence coverage (including dependency paths) offsets lower utilization; transformed stores leave an accuracy gap even with full access. Establishes naive RAG as an essential baseline before complex agentic memory. Strong independent X signal from @omarsar0 (bookmarks ~980). Contrast: complex agentic memory is not always better than raw-history RAG on conversation tasks. Heat 5. Concept written.

Certified Execution Harness (arXiv:2610.31376, submitted 22 Oct, Gu & Zhu): formalizes the harness between LLM reasoning and tools as a Petri-net supervisory control problem. Marks represent capabilities, approvals, and budgets; transitions fire only when required marks are present, refusing injected requests with unmet or spent preconditions regardless of wording. Maximally permissive supervisor transfers safety under four assumptions; least-intrusive refinement characterized for replanning closed loops. X discussion @deeponailabs. Signal for formal safety between reasoning and action. Heat 4. Concept written.

Federated GraphRAG (arXiv:2610.30728, submitted 22 Oct, Wang et al.): FedGRAG enables distributed GraphRAG across privacy-constrained clients via Federated Knowledge Routing (lightweight indices, no local graph exposure) and controlled-disclosure evidence abstracts. Outperforms baselines on retrieval and generation. Continues GraphRAG / context-graph theme under federation constraints. Heat 4.

Subgoal-aware Memory Graph (arXiv:2610.31307, submitted 22 Oct, Luo et al.): for GUI agents, distills trajectories into intent-level subgoal nodes in a graph (merge/extend/retrieve by observation). Improves SPA-Bench success rate +11.11% over baseline. Finer than single actions, coarser than full trajectories for cross-task reuse. Heat 4.

MemPABench (arXiv:2610.30485, submitted 21 Oct): interactive benchmark for personal agent memory on interaction preferences (when to clarify/confirm/act) rather than pure factual recall. Across four memory systems and four backbones, retrieval-based systems do not beat No Memory on preference selection; file-based memory over-applies. Highlights gap between factual and preference memory. Heat 3.
